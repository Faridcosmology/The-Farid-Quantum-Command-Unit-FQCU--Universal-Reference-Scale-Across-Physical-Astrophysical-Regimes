Abstract

Measuring phenomena across quantum physics, molecular biology, spacecraft engineering, and observational astrophysics typically requires switching between disconnected unit systems—such as electron-volts for subatomic particles, kilojoules per mole for cellular metabolism, joules for spacecraft mechanics, and ergs or solar luminosities for cosmic events. Within the Tanfarid Quantum Thermodynamic Universe (TQTU) framework, the Farid Quantum Command Unit (FQCU) is proposed as a standardized metrological reference scale anchored to the discrete energy quantum of a \(1\text{ Terahertz}\) (\(1\text{ THz}\)) photon (\(1\text{ FQCU} = h\nu_0 \approx 6.62607 \times 10^{-22}\text{ J} \approx 4.13567\text{ meV}\)). Using this single electromagnetic quantum and its coupled temporal (\(T_0 = 1\text{ ps}\)) and spatial (\(\lambda_0 \approx 0.300\text{ mm}\)) benchmarks, this paper maps laboratory systems alongside verified NASA mission datasets—including Cosmic Background Explorer (COBE/FIRAS) cosmic microwave background and interstellar \([{\rm C\,II}]\) spectra, Solar Radiation and Climate Experiment (SORCE) solar irradiance, Parker Solar Probe perihelion kinematics, and ultra-high-energy cosmic rays—spanning 68 orders of magnitude (\(10^{-3}\) to \(10^{65}\text{ FQCU}\)) on one continuous numerical axis.

1. Introduction: The Fragmentation of Scientific Scales

Modern science relies on specialized measurement conventions tailored to narrow disciplinary regimes:
• Radio & Microwave Cosmology relies on gigahertz (\(\text{GHz}\)), millikelvin brightness temperatures (\(\text{mK}\)), or inverse centimeters (\(\text{cm}^{-1}\)).
• Subatomic and Particle Physics relies on electron-volts (\(\text{eV}, \text{MeV}, \text{GeV}, \text{EeV}\)).
• Chemistry and Cellular Biology relies on kilojoules per mole (\(\text{kJ/mol}\)), kilocalories, or thermal energy units (\(k_{B}T\)).
• Spacecraft Engineering and Astrophysics relies on Joules (\(\text{J}\)), Watts (\(\text{W}\)), ergs (\(10^{-7}\text{ J}\)), or nominal solar luminosities (\(L_{\odot }\)).
While mathematically convertible, these fragmented units obscure direct numerical comparisons between biological energy budgets, subatomic rest masses, spacecraft orbital mechanics, and stellar radiation. Establishing a unified quantum reference benchmark allows researchers to index vastly different physical regimes onto a single, continuous logarithmic scale.

2. Exact SI Mathematical Definition of the FQCU

Under the revised International System of Units (SI), both the Planck constant (\(h\)) and the speed of light (\(c\)) are exact defined constants. The FQCU is defined through Planck’s energy–frequency relation (\(E = h\nu\)), anchored to a reference frequency of \(\nu_0 = 1\text{ THz} = 10^{12}\text{ Hz}\):
\(1\text{\ FQCU}\equiv E_{0}=h\cdot \nu _{0}=(6.62607015\times 10^{-34}\text{\ J}\cdot \text{s})\times (10^{12}\text{\ Hz})=6.62607015\times 10^{-22}\text{\ J}\)
Using the exact SI elementary charge (\(e = 1.602176634 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}\)), one FQCU in electron-volts is:
\(1\text{\ FQCU}=\frac{6.62607015\times 10^{-22}\text{\ J}}{1.602176634\times 10^{-19}\text{\ J/eV}}\approx 4.1356677\times 10^{-3}\text{\ eV}\approx 4.13567\text{\ meV}\)

Why a \(1\text{ THz}\) Photon?

1. Physical Quantum Packet: Unlike a bare frequency value—which counts oscillations per second—a \(1\text{ THz}\) photon represents a discrete packet of electromagnetic energy.
2. Mesoscopic Bridge: \(1\text{ THz}\) sits at the physical boundary between classical high-frequency electronics and quantum photonics, matching the natural rotational and collective vibrational modes of water networks and biological macromolecules.
3. 3. Coupled Space, Time, and Power Benchmarks

Pairing \(\nu_0 = 10^{12}\text{ Hz}\) with the exact speed of light (\(c = 299{,}792{,}458\text{ m/s}\)) establishes coupled reference units for time, length, and radiative power:

\(T_{0}=\frac{1}{\nu _{0}}=10^{-12}\text{\ s}=1\text{\ picosecond\ (ps)}\)
• Baseline Spatial Unit (\(\lambda _{0}\)): The free-space wavelength of a \(1\text{ THz}\) photon:
\(\lambda _{0}=\frac{c}{\nu _{0}}=2.99792458\times 10^{-4}\text{\ m}\approx 0.2998\text{\ mm}\)

\(1\text{\ FQCU/s}=6.62607015\times 10^{-22}\text{\ W}\)

5. Methodological Scope and Scientific Context

When applying the FQCU across laboratory and astrophysical datasets, three methodological boundaries must be maintained:
1. Metrological Ruler vs. Fundamental Natural Limit: Unlike Planck units—which combine \(c\), \(\hbar \), and Newton’s gravitational constant (\(G\)) to mark the theoretical scale of quantum gravity (\(E_P \approx 1.956 \times 10^{9}\text{ J} \approx 2.95 \times 10^{30}\text{ FQCU}\))—the FQCU intentionally omits \(G\). Rather than representing a fundamental physical limit of spacetime, it functions as a human- and molecule-scaled metrological ruler anchored to a \(1\text{ THz}\) photon.
2. Cross-Disciplinary Budget Accounting: Expressing NASA’s COBE/FIRAS interstellar \([{\rm C\,II}]\) cooling photons (\(1.90\text{ FQCU}\)), cellular ATP hydrolysis (\(76.4\text{ FQCU}\)), Higgs boson rest energy (\(3.03 \times 10^{13}\text{ FQCU}\)), and solar radiative output (\(5.78 \times 10^{47}\text{ FQCU/s}\)) in a single unit provides a transparent accounting axis for interdisciplinary modeling within the TQTU framework without switching unit conventions.
3. Empirical Verification Boundary: As a unit convention, the FQCU is exact by SI definition (\(1\text{ FQCU} \equiv h \times 10^{12}\text{ Hz}\)). Any deeper dynamical hypotheses within the TQTU or Farid Quantum Physics programmes—such as nonperturbative quantum rotor couplings or sub-quantum lattice dynamics—remain separate physical models subject to independent numerical validation and peer-reviewed empirical testing.
